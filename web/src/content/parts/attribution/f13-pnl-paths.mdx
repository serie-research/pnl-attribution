Leave-one-out removes each signal and reruns the remaining portfolio at the
same risk target. The Grinold–Easton spread and position-limit lines are
attributed portfolios, each net of its allocated costs, so the spread line is
not the cash spread paid.

<WhatToNotice>

With a spread of 2.0 std(G), slow A, fast B and slow B never trade alone,
because their forecasts stay inside the no-trade band. Fast A trades alone on
only four dates and ends with a small loss, and its P&L is slightly negatively
correlated with the portfolio's, so any positive weight would worsen the
least-squares fit. Both proxy methods therefore give zero to every signal and
leave the whole P&L in the residual.

Together, though, the signals clear the spread. This is the voting mechanism
Durin describes (section 3.3), and his split gives the resulting P&L to all four
signals on this path.

With the tight position limit, Grinold–Easton's negative spread and
position-limit portfolios offset large positive signal portfolios. Their sum
tracks the same total as Durin's four signal paths, which include these
effects. Under this limit the risk target is out of reach, and the two Shapley
games give the same paths.

At high innovation correlation, the iterated fit keeps only fast A and leaves a
positive residual. The fit converges on that one proxy without recovering the
other signals' contributions. The next figure repeats the comparison across
seeds.
